# UNIT1-BIOSAFETY-Regulatory framework for GMOs in India

## 1.1 Regulatory framework in India governing GMOs

India regulates GMOs mainly under:

- **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**
- **Rules, 1989** — Rules for the Manufacture, Use, Import, Export and Storage of Hazardous Microorganisms/Genetically Engineered Organisms or Cells.

The main purpose is to make sure that biotechnology research, field trials and commercial release are scientifically checked for safety.

### Main authorities involved

1. **Institutional Biosafety Committee (IBSC)**
   - Works at the institution/laboratory level.
   - Reviews and monitors GMO research.

2. **Review Committee on Genetic Manipulation (RCGM)**
   - Works under DBT.
   - Monitors ongoing GMO/rDNA research.
   - Reviews higher-risk work and confined field trials.

3. **Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee (GEAC)**
   - Apex statutory authority for environmental approval.
   - Deals with large-scale trials, environmental release and commercialization.

4. **Recombinant DNA Advisory Committee (RDAC)**
   - Gives scientific and policy advice.
   - Recommends biosafety guidelines.

5. **State Biotechnology Coordination Committee (SBCC)**
   - Monitors GMO activities at state level.
   - Can inspect and investigate violations.

6. **District Level Committee (DLC)**
   - Monitors activities at district/local level.
   - Deals with inspections and emergencies.

### Regulation for Genetically Modified (GM) Foods

GM food and feed are regulated under the **Rules, 1989** and the **Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006**.

**FSSAI** is responsible for setting and implementing science-based food safety standards, including GM foods.

# GENETICALLY MODIFIED ORGANISMS AND THEIR AGRICULTURAL APPLICATIONS

### Definition

A **Genetically Modified Organism (GMO)** is a plant, animal or microorganism whose DNA has been changed using genetic engineering to give it a desired characteristic or to make a desired biological product.

### Important points

- GMO modification is done using modern biotechnology.
- Important terms include:
  - Genetic engineering
  - Recombinant DNA technology
  - Gene technology
  - Genetic modification

### Examples of GM traits

**Herbicide tolerance (HT):**
- Crop is modified so that a particular herbicide can kill weeds without damaging the crop.
- Example: glyphosate-tolerant soybean and maize.

**Insect resistance (IR):**
- Crop is modified to produce proteins that kill or control particular insect pests.
- Example: Bt cotton and Bt maize.
- Bt genes come from **Bacillus thuringiensis**.

### GM crops globally

Major GM-crop producing countries include:

- USA
- Brazil
- Argentina
- Canada

### GM crops in India

- Bt cotton has been widely adopted.
- GM food crops such as HT mustard/DMH-11 have caused legal and social debates.

# Regulation for Genetically Modified (GM) Foods

GM foods and feed produced using genetic engineering require regulatory approval.

The major legal framework includes:

- Environment (Protection) Act, 1986
- Rules, 1989
- Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006
- FSSAI regulations

---

# Key Features of the Food Safety and Standards (Genetically Modified or Engineered Foods) Regulations, 2021

- Applies to GMOs intended for food use.
- Prior approval is required for manufacture, sale and import of GM food.
- Also applies to ingredients obtained from GMOs.
- Covers ingredients:
  - containing modified DNA, and
  - not containing detectable modified DNA but made using GMO-derived ingredients/additives/processing aids.

### GM food approval

A person cannot manufacture, store, distribute, sell or import GM food/food ingredients without prior approval from the Food Authority.

### GM Food Labelling

Food containing **1% or more of an individual GM ingredient** must be labelled.

Label:

> "Contains GMO/Ingredients derived from GMO"

The labelling requirement does not apply where modified DNA is not detectable, as described in the regulations.

### Infant food

GMOs/GE organisms/LMOs must not be used as ingredients in infant food.

---

# Procedure for Grant of Prior Approval of FSSAI: Key Points

### If the food contains LMOs

- First obtain environmental-safety approval from GEAC.
- Then submit the application to the Food Authority in the prescribed form.

### If the food does not contain LMOs

- Application can be submitted directly to the Food Authority in the prescribed form.

### After approval

- Food business operator must obtain the required food-business licence.
- If the operator believes the food creates a health risk:
  - immediately stop manufacture/import/sale/distribution,
  - recall the food according to food-recall rules.

### Complaints

Food Safety Officers/Designated Officers must inform the Food Authority about complaints concerning approved GM food.

### Unique identification

Once a GMO/GE organism/LMO with a unique identification code has been approved by FSSAI, another food business operator does not need a separate approval for the same approved GMO.

# The GM Mustard Imbroglio (Gene Campaign v. Union of India)

The case concerns **Dhara Mustard Hybrid-11 (DMH-11)**, a GM mustard developed by Delhi University.

### Main points

- GEAC gave conditional environmental clearance to DMH-11.
- Anti-GM groups and environmentalists challenged the approval.

### The Split Verdict

The Supreme Court gave a **split 1:1 verdict**.

### Justice B.V. Nagarathna

- Considered GEAC's approval invalid.
- Criticised dependence on foreign research data.
- Wanted stronger Indian/local studies on health and environmental effects.

### Justice Sanjay Karol

- Found no arbitrary problem in GEAC's approach.
- Supported trials with strict safeguards.

### Directive

Because of the disagreement:

- Matter was referred to a three-judge Bench.
- Government was directed to formulate a comprehensive National Policy on GM crops after consulting:
  - States
  - Scientists
  - Farmers

# 1.2 Recombinant DNA Advisory Committee (RDAC)

## Introduction

RDAC is one of the earliest committees in India's biosafety system.

Its main role is **scientific advice and policy recommendations** related to:

- Recombinant DNA technology
- Biotechnology
- Biosafety
- GMO research

## Establishment

- Constituted under the **Department of Biotechnology (DBT)**.
- DBT comes under the Ministry of Science and Technology.
- Legal basis: **Rules, 1989** under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986.

## Objectives of RDAC

RDAC aims to:

- Advise the Government on developments in rDNA technology.
- Recommend safety regulations.
- Review Indian and international biosafety guidelines.
- Promote safe use of GMOs.
- Suggest changes in regulations as science develops.

## Composition

Experts may come from:

- Molecular Biology
- Biotechnology
- Genetics
- Microbiology
- Medicine
- Agriculture
- Environmental Sciences
- Toxicology
- Government departments

Usually chaired by a senior scientist nominated by DBT.

## Major Functions of RDAC

### 1. Policy Advisory Role

Advises Government on:

- rDNA research
- Genetic engineering
- Biotechnology policies
- Biosafety

### 2. Formulation of Biosafety Guidelines

Recommends guidelines for:

- Laboratory biosafety
- GMO research
- Containment
- rDNA products
- Risk assessment

### 3. Monitoring Scientific Developments

Reviews new technologies such as:

- CRISPR
- Gene editing
- Synthetic biology
- Genome engineering

Then suggests suitable regulatory changes.

### 4. Coordination

Coordinates with:

- DBT
- IBSC
- RCGM
- GEAC

### 5. Advising on Risk Assessment

Considers:

- Environmental risks
- Human health risks
- Animal health risks
- Food safety

## Nature of RDAC

**RDAC is NOT a regulatory authority.**

It is:

- Advisory committee
- Scientific advisory group
- Policy-support body

It **does not approve or reject GMO projects**.

## Powers under RDAC

### Can:

- Recommend
- Advise
- Suggest amendments
- Formulate biosafety principles

### Cannot:

- Grant approvals
- Issue licences
- Conduct inspections
- Enforce regulations

## Relationship with Other Regulatory Bodies

### 1. Institutional Biosafety Committee (IBSC)

- Present in research institutions.
- Monitors laboratory-level GMO research.
- Reports to RCGM.

### 2. Review Committee on Genetic Manipulation (RCGM)

- Works under DBT.
- Monitors ongoing GMO research.
- Handles confined field trials.

### 3. Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee (GEAC)

- Apex approval authority.
- Works under MoEF&CC.
- Deals with:
  - Large-scale use
  - Environmental release
  - Commercialization

### 4. State Biotechnology Coordination Committee (SBCC)

- State-level monitoring.

### 5. District Level Committee (DLC)

- Local inspections.
- Checks biosafety compliance.

## Importance of RDAC

RDAC helps in:

- Developing India's biosafety framework.
- Promoting responsible biotechnology.
- Connecting scientific progress with regulations.
- Supporting safe rDNA technology.
- Harmonizing Indian rules with international developments.

## Limitations

- Only advisory.
- No statutory approval power.
- Its recommendations must be implemented through bodies such as RCGM and GEAC.
- Depends on Government acceptance.

# Review Committee on Genetic Manipulation (RCGM)

### Definition

RCGM is an expert body under DBT that monitors recombinant-DNA research and handles higher-risk research and confined/small-scale field trials of genetically engineered organisms.

### Main role

RCGM works mainly at the **research and confined-trial stage**.

### Important functions

- Monitors ongoing GMO/rDNA research.
- Reviews biosafety of higher-risk work.
- Checks whether proper containment is available.
- Issues guidelines for laboratory and greenhouse containment.
- Handles permitted import/export of GE organisms/cells for research.
- Authorizes biosafety-data generation.
- Approves confined/small-scale field trials.
- Monitors trials through committees such as MECs.

### RCGM vs GEAC

**RCGM = research + data + confined trials**

**GEAC = environmental release + large-scale use + commercialization**

This distinction is extremely important for exams.

### Examples of RCGM involvement

RCGM has been involved in reviewing:

- Bt cotton
- Bt brinjal
- GM mustard/DMH-11
- Recombinant vaccine candidates
- Gene-therapy proposals
- Industrial biotechnology proposals

### Limitation

RCGM **cannot approve commercial cultivation or marketing**.

# REGISTRATION OF IBSC AT IBKP

Registration of IBSC through the **Indian Biosafety Knowledge Portal (IBKP)** is mandatory for organizations covered by Rules, 1989.

Without valid registration/renewal, the organization is not eligible to conduct regulated rDNA/hazardous-microorganism activities.

### Step 1 - Organisation registration

- Organization registers online on IBKP.
- Details are verified.
- Permanent login ID is provided.

### Step 2 - IBSC registration

Using the organization's login:

- Submit IBSC registration.
- Provide biodata and consent of members.
- Include DBT nominee details.
- Provide proposed research activities.
- Provide information about containment facilities.

The submitted information is verified and the decision is communicated through the portal.

Main responsibilities:

- Ensure biosafety measures prevent accidental release.
- Monitor BSL-3/BSL-4 facilities where applicable.

- Help prepare emergency plans.

Responsibilities:

- Review ongoing activities.
- Guide IBSC on biosafety.

- Participate in IBSC meetings.

## Internal and External Members

- Review applications using guidelines/checklists.
- Participate in biosafety/biosecurity training.

- Minimum **2 meetings per year**.
- For work involving **Risk Group 3 and above** or **Category III and above** experiments:

- Hazardous microorganism incidents
- rDNA-related emergencies

Minutes must:

# CLASSIFICATION OF GENETIC ENGINEERING EXPERIMENTS

Genetic engineering experiments are divided into:

- Category I
- Category II
- Category III

The risk generally increases from **Category I → Category II → Category III**.

## CATEGORY - I

Work involving defined genes/DNA from microbial, plant or animal sources that are generally considered safe for humans, animals and plants.

### Examples/characteristics

- Routine rDNA laboratory experiments.
- Work in contained laboratories/greenhouses.
- Does not normally include contained limited field trials.

### Approval

**IBSC can approve.**

---

## CATEGORY - II

Work involving defined genes/DNA that are generally non-pathogenic but may have implications for:

- Plants
- Insects
- Other organisms/environment

### Approval

**IBSC can approve with intimation to RCGM**, as applicable under the guidelines.

---

## CATEGORY - III

Work involving genetic material that may cause changes in the biosphere or does not fall under Category I or II.

Includes higher-risk work such as:

- Certain field experiments
- Work involving higher-risk organisms
- Experiments with greater environmental implications

### Approval

**RCGM approval is required before the experiment.**

### Easy memory

**Category I → IBSC**

**Category II → IBSC + information to RCGM**

**Category III → RCGM approval**

MEC monitors field experiments involving transgenic plants.

### Main functions

- Visit experimental field sites.
- Review trial design.
- Review field data.
- Collect information about agronomic advantages.
- Assess risks and benefits.
- Help analyse environmental-risk data.
- Recommend environmentally safe and economically useful crops to RCGM/GEAC.

# Amendments in the Guidelines

## Defining small experimental field trials

Small experimental field trials:

- Total maximum: **20 acres** at multiple locations in one crop season.
- Maximum at one location: **1 acre**.
- Experiments exceeding these limits require GEAC approval.

## Large scale experiments

Research using fermentors above **20 litres**, when used only to produce enough material for research/pre-clinical studies, is not automatically treated as large-scale commercial experimentation under the described provision.

# Protocol-1 For New Transgenic Event

### Stepwise process

1. **IBSC**
   - Prepares/submits application data.

2. **RCGM**
   - Evaluates application data.
   - Approves laboratory, greenhouse and contained field trials.
   - Requires environmental, toxicity and allergenicity data.

3. **MEC**
   - Evaluates/monitors contained field trials.

4. **GEAC**
   - Approves large-scale field trials.
   - Reviews data.
   - Evaluates environmental safety.

5. **Ministry of Agriculture / DAC/ICAR**
   - Deals with commercial release, notification and registration.

6. **Ministry of Agriculture + State Governments**
   - Post-release monitoring and vigilance.

### Easy sequence

**IBSC → RCGM → MEC → GEAC → Agriculture authorities → Post-release monitoring**

# Protocol-II: For Released Event/ Gene

For an already released event/gene:

1. IBSC prepares/submits data.
2. RCGM verifies the case and biosafety.
3. GEAC approves large-scale field trials where required.
4. Field trials are conducted.
5. GEAC evaluates environmental data.
6. DAC/ICAR/Ministry of Agriculture deals with commercial release/registration.
7. Post-release monitoring is carried out.

# Biosafety parameters on Transgenic crops

## B. Biosafety Parameters:

### 1. Genetic and Molecular parameters

Check:

- Number of gene inserts/copy number.
- Stability of inserted gene.
- Level of gene expression.
- Location of expression.
- Duration of expression.
- Characteristics of gene product.
- Effectiveness of gene product.
- Composition of the modified crop.

### 2. Environmental parameters

Check:

- Gene flow.
- Effects of out-crossing.
- Effects on target organisms.
- Effects on non-target organisms.
- Effects on soil microorganisms/soil biota.

### 3. Toxicity parameters including histo-pathological studies (need based)

Food/feed safety may be evaluated using:

- Small laboratory animals
- Livestock
- Birds
- Fish

### 4. Allergenicity parameters (need based)

Possible tests include:

- Primary skin irritation.
- Mucous membrane irritation.
- Immunological responses in suitable animal systems.

# Criteria's for standard safety tests:

Safety assessment of GM foods may include:

### 1. Study introduced DNA

Check:

- Introduced DNA
- New proteins
- New metabolites produced

### 2. Chemical composition

Compare relevant plant parts for:

- Nutrients
- Anti-nutrients
- Natural toxins
- Known allergens

### 3. Gene transfer

Assess the possible risk of genes moving from food to microorganisms in the human gut.

### 4. Amount consumed

Estimate how much of the GM food would normally be present in the human diet.

### 5. Compare with conventional food

Compare safety/nutritional findings with equivalent conventional foods.

### 6. Additional animal studies

If there is a possible health risk, additional animal toxicity studies may be required.

### Easy memory

**DNA → Protein → Composition → Gene transfer → Amount eaten → Compare → Animal tests if needed**

- Safety equipment

- Facility design
- Operational practices

### Biological containment

Makes a GE organism:

- Unable to survive normally outside controlled conditions, or
- Much less able to survive/reproduce in the environment.
