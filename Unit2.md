# UNIT -II BIOETHICS AND BIOSAFETY

## Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)

### Definition

The Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) is an international legally binding treaty.

It aims to:
- Conserve biological diversity.
- Ensure the sustainable use of biological resources.
- Ensure fair and equal sharing of benefits obtained from genetic resources.

**Adopted:** 5 June 1992 at Earth Summit, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil  
**Entered into force:** 29 December 1993

---

## Objectives of the CBD :

The Convention has three main objectives:

### 1. Conservation of Biological Diversity
- Protect ecosystems, species, and genetic diversity.
- Prevent the loss of biodiversity.

### 2. Sustainable Use of Biological Resources
- Use biological resources responsibly.
- Make sure resources are not damaged or exhausted for future generations.
- Promote sustainable agriculture, forestry, and fisheries.

### 3. Fair and Equitable Sharing of Benefits
- Benefits obtained from genetic resources should be shared fairly between countries and communities.
- Benefits may include:
  - Research results
  - Technology
  - Financial benefits

---

## Principles of CBD :

- **Conservation of biodiversity is a global responsibility.**
  - Protecting biodiversity is the responsibility of the whole world.

- **Countries have sovereign rights over their biological resources.**
  - Each country has control over its own biological resources.

- **Sustainable development should balance environmental protection and economic growth.**
  - Development should happen without unnecessarily harming the environment.

- **Public participation and awareness are encouraged.**
  - People should be informed and involved in biodiversity protection.

- **International cooperation is essential.**
  - Countries need to work together to protect biodiversity.

---

## Key Features :

- Conservation of ecosystems, species, and genetic resources.
- Sustainable use of biological resources.
- Protection of endangered species.
- Access to genetic resources through prior informed consent.
- Fair sharing of benefits from biotechnology and genetic resources.
- Promotion of research, education, and public awareness.
- Financial and technical support for developing countries.

---

## Importance of CBD :

- Protects biodiversity worldwide.
- Helps prevent extinction of plants and animals.
- Supports sustainable development.
- Encourages cooperation between countries.
- Provides the legal framework for agreements such as:
  - Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety
  - Nagoya Protocol

---

## Limitations :

- Implementation is not equal in all countries.
- Biodiversity loss is still continuing because of:
  - Habitat destruction
  - Climate change
  - Pollution
  - Invasive species
- Some developing countries have limited:
  - Funding
  - Technical knowledge
  - Technical capacity


# Cartagena Protocol

### Definition

The Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety is an international treaty that ensures the **safe handling, transport, and use of Living Modified Organisms (LMOs).**

Important components include:
- Advance Informed Agreement (AIA)
- Risk Assessment
- Biosafety Clearing-House (BCH)

**Adopted:** 29 January 2000  
**Entered into force:** 11 September 2003

---

## Core Features and Rules:

### Advance Informed Agreement (AIA)
- Exporters must obtain prior consent from importing countries.
- Applies before shipping LMOs that are intended to be introduced into the environment.

### Precautionary Approach
- Governments can take preventive action even when there is not complete scientific certainty about possible risks to:
  - Biodiversity
  - Human health

### Biosafety Clearing-House (BCH)
- An information-sharing platform.
- Helps countries exchange:
  - Scientific data
  - Technical information
  - Legal information
  - Information about LMOs

### Scope and Exemptions
- Mainly focuses on LMOs such as:
  - Seeds
  - Trees
  - Fish
  - Other LMOs intended for environmental release
- Also covers farm commodities intended for food or feed.
- Excludes:
  - Human pharmaceuticals
  - Processed derivatives


## Objectives :

- Protect biological diversity from harmful effects of LMOs.
- Ensure safe transfer, handling, and use of LMOs produced using modern biotechnology.
- Protect human health while conserving biodiversity.
- Regulate international movement (import/export) of LMOs.
- Help importing countries make informed decisions before accepting LMOs.
- Apply the precautionary principle.
  - Countries can restrict or prohibit LMOs when scientific evidence about risks is uncertain.
- Strengthen international cooperation and information sharing about biosafety.


## Salient Features :

### Advance Informed Agreement (AIA)
- Exporters must notify and obtain consent from importing countries.
- Applies to the first intentional transboundary movement of certain LMOs.

### Risk Assessment
- LMOs are scientifically evaluated for possible risks to:
  - Biodiversity
  - Human health

### Risk Management
- Countries should take measures to prevent or reduce identified risks from LMOs.

### Precautionary Principle
- A country can refuse or restrict LMO imports even when scientific evidence is incomplete if serious harm is possible.

### Biosafety Clearing-House (BCH)
- An international online platform for sharing:
  - Biosafety laws
  - Regulations
  - Risk assessments
  - Decisions related to LMOs

### Documentation and Labeling
- LMOs crossing international borders must be:
  - Properly identified
  - Documented
  - Labeled

### Capacity Building
- Helps developing countries improve:
  - Biosafety infrastructure
  - Technical expertise
  - Regulatory systems

### Public Awareness and Participation
- Encourages public access to biosafety information.
- Encourages people to participate in decision-making.

### Compliance Mechanism
- Parties must follow the Protocol.
- They must also report their implementation measures.
- # Advance Informed Agreement (AIA) Procedure

### Definition

The Advance Informed Agreement (AIA) is an important procedure under the Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety.

It ensures that before the **first intentional transboundary movement** of certain Living Modified Organisms (LMOs) intended for environmental introduction:

- The importing country receives complete information.
- The importing country gives its consent.

In simple words:

**Exporter informs → Importing country assesses → Importing country gives/denies consent → LMO can be moved accordingly.**

---

## Objectives :

- Protect biodiversity and human health.
- Ensure informed decision-making by importing countries.
- Assess possible environmental risks before import.
- Promote transparency in international trade of LMOs.


## Steps in the AIA Procedure :

### 1. Notification

- The exporter/exporting country sends a written notification to the importing country's competent authority.

The notification includes:
- Name and identity of the LMO.
- Intended use.
- Quantity to be exported.
- Risk assessment information.
- Safe handling, storage, and transport measures.

### 2. Acknowledgement of Receipt

- The importing country acknowledges that it has received the notification.
- This is generally done within **90 days**.
- It confirms that the required information has been received.

### 3. Risk Assessment

- The importing country evaluates possible risks of the LMO to:
  - Biodiversity
  - Human health
- The assessment is based on:
  - Scientific evidence
  - Internationally accepted principles

### 4. Decision by the Importing Country

The importing country may:

- Approve the import.
- Approve the import with specific conditions.
- Request additional information.
- Reject the import if the LMO creates an unacceptable risk.

### 5. Communication of Decision

- The importing country informs the exporter about its decision.
- The decision is recorded in the **Biosafety Clearing-House (BCH).**

### 6. Review of Decision

A decision can be reviewed when:
- New scientific evidence becomes available.
- Circumstances change.


## Importance of AIA :

- Prevents unsafe movement of LMOs.
- Protects ecosystems and biodiversity.
- Promotes international cooperation.
- Ensures transparency and accountability.
- Supports science-based decision-making while applying the precautionary principle.


# Risk Assessment

### Definition

Risk assessment is the **scientific process of evaluating the possible harmful effects of Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs) or Living Modified Organisms (LMOs) on human health and the environment before they are approved for use or release.**

In simple words:

**Risk Assessment = Find the hazard → Study exposure → Determine the overall risk.**

---

## Objectives :

- Identify possible hazards.
- Find out how likely harmful effects are.
- Determine how serious the possible effects may be.
- Help authorities make regulatory decisions.


## Steps in Risk Assessment :

### 1. Hazard Identification
- Identify possible harmful effects of the GMO.

**Question:** What can go wrong?

### 2. Hazard Characterization
- Determine the nature and seriousness of the identified hazard.

**Question:** How harmful can it be?

### 3. Exposure Assessment
- Estimate the probability and level of exposure to the hazard.

**Question:** How much exposure can occur and how likely is it?

### 4. Risk Characterization
- Combine hazard and exposure information.
- Determine the overall risk.

**Question:** What is the actual overall risk?

### 5. Risk Evaluation
- Decide whether the risk is acceptable.
- If not, decide whether control measures are required.

**Question:** Is the risk acceptable or does it need control?


## Importance :

- Protects biodiversity.
- Protects human and animal health.
- Supports evidence-based regulatory decisions.
- Ensures safe use of biotechnology.


# Risk Management

### Definition

Risk management is the process of using measures to **prevent, reduce, or control risks identified during risk assessment.**

In simple words:

**Risk Assessment finds the risk → Risk Management controls the risk.**

---

## Objectives :

- Minimize environmental and health risks.
- Ensure safe use, storage, and disposal of GMOs.
- Prevent accidental release.
- Monitor long-term effects.


## Risk Management Measures :

- **Containment and biosafety practices**
  - Keep GMOs safely controlled.

- **Controlled field trials**
  - Test GMOs under controlled conditions.

- **Monitoring and surveillance**
  - Continuously observe for possible harmful effects.

- **Emergency response plans**
  - Prepare actions to take if an accident or release occurs.

- **Staff training and awareness**
  - Train workers about proper GMO safety procedures.

- **Proper waste disposal and decontamination**
  - Safely dispose of GMO waste and remove contamination.
  - # Handling of GMOs

### Definition

Proper handling of GMOs is necessary to prevent:
- Accidental exposure.
- Accidental environmental release.


## Guidelines :

- Handle GMOs only by trained personnel.
- Use appropriate **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE).**
- Follow **Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).**
- Prevent spills and contamination.
- Store GMOs in secure and designated areas.
- Dispose of GMO waste according to biosafety regulations.


# Transport of GMOs

### Definition

GMOs must be transported safely to prevent:
- Leakage.
- Accidental release.


## Requirements :

- Use leak-proof and durable containers.
- Follow national and international biosafety regulations.
- Maintain appropriate storage conditions during transport.
- Carry emergency response instructions.
- Ensure transport personnel are properly trained.


# Packaging of GMOs

### Definition

Packaging protects GMOs during:
- Storage
- Transport

It helps prevent damage, leakage, and contamination.


## Requirements :

- Use strong, sealed, and leak-proof containers.
- Prevent damage and contamination.
- Packaging should be suitable for the type of GMO.
- Follow biosafety standards.
- Include all required documentation.


# Identification (Labeling) of GMOs

### Definition

Proper identification/labeling helps ensure:
- Traceability.
- Regulatory compliance.
- Safe handling of GMOs.


## Label Information :

The label should contain:

- **Name and identity of the GMO.**
- A statement that the shipment contains **Living Modified Organisms (LMOs).**
- **Intended use**, such as:
  - Research
  - Cultivation
  - Food
  - Feed
  - Processing
- Handling and storage instructions.
- Contact details of the exporter or responsible authority.
- Emergency contact information, if applicable.


## Importance :

- Enables traceability.
- Ensures safe handling.
- Supports regulatory compliance.
- Helps in emergency response.
- Protects human health and the environment.


# Advance Informed Agreement (AIA) Procedure

## Definition

The AIA procedure ensures that an importing country gets complete information and gives consent before the **first intentional transboundary movement** of certain LMOs intended for introduction into the environment.

### Scope of the AIA Procedure

The AIA procedure applies to:

- The **first intentional transboundary movement** of LMOs.
- The LMOs must be intended for **intentional introduction into the environment of the importing country.**

### Examples

- GM crop seeds for cultivation.
- GM trees for plantation.
- GM fish intended for release into water bodies.


## LMOs Exempt from AIA

AIA does **not** apply to:

- LMOs intended for **direct use as food, feed, or processing (LMO-FFPs).**
- LMOs in transit through another country.
- LMOs intended for contained use, such as laboratory research under appropriate containment.
- LMOs identified by the Parties as unlikely to have adverse effects.


## Steps in the AIA Procedure

### 1. Notification by the Exporter

Before the first shipment, the exporter/exporting Party must notify the competent authority of the importing country.

The notification should include:
- Identity and characteristics of the LMO.
- Intended use.
- Quantity to be exported.
- Method of transport.
- Proposed risk management measures.
- Available risk assessment information.

### 2. Acknowledgement of Receipt

- The importing country acknowledges receipt of the notification.
- This is generally done within **90 days**.
- It indicates whether the information is complete.

### 3. Risk Assessment

The importing country performs a scientific risk assessment to evaluate possible impacts on:

- Biodiversity.
- Human health, where relevant.
- Non-target organisms.
- Ecosystems.

### 4. Decision by the Importing Country

The importing country may:

- Approve the import.
- Approve it with conditions such as containment or monitoring.
- Request additional information.
- Prohibit the import if risks are unacceptable.

The decision is generally communicated within **270 days** of receiving the notification.

### 5. Communication Through the Biosafety Clearing-House

- The final decision is communicated to the exporter.
- The decision is made available through the **Biosafety Clearing-House (BCH).**
- This improves transparency and information sharing between Parties.


## Information Required in the Notification

The exporter should provide:

- Name and identity of the LMO.
- Details of the genetic modification.
- Intended use.
- Quantity and destination.
- Risk assessment reports.
- Safe handling, storage, transport, and disposal procedures.
- Emergency response measures, if applicable.


## Role of Risk Assessment

Risk assessment evaluates:

- Potential harmful effects on biodiversity.
- Likelihood of gene flow to wild relatives.
- Effects on non-target organisms.
- Persistence and invasiveness.
- Possible impacts on human and animal health.


## Importance of the AIA Procedure

- Prevents unintentional environmental harm.
- Strengthens biosafety governance.
- Supports informed national decision-making.
- Encourages transparency and international cooperation.
- Reflects the precautionary approach in biotechnology regulation.
